# Leasing implementation decisions

1. Each lease may have one security-deposit schedule. Creating it also creates one linked AED draft invoice using the existing `refundable_deposit` accounting treatment. For VAT-enabled organizations the deposit invoice is out of scope with zero VAT.
2. Collection uses the existing invoice post and payment workflows. Posting debits Accounts Receivable (1100) and credits Customer Deposits (2300); receipt uses the existing Undeposited Funds and bank-reconciliation workflow. The lease-compliance page derives collected value and status from the linked invoice and payments.
3. Creating a deposit does not automatically activate or block a lease. Deposit refunds, deductions, and forfeitures remain excluded until their accounting mappings and approval rules are explicitly approved.
4. Post-dated cheques are operational records and create no journal entries. Each cheque belongs to one lease and progresses from scheduled to deposited, then cleared or bounced. A bounce requires a dated reason. Only bounced cheques may be replaced; replacement creates a new scheduled cheque linked to the immutable original, which is marked replaced. Cheque numbers are unique within an organization, and all actions are audited and tenant scoped.
5. Lease service charges are explicit lease records linked to draft revenue invoices. Each charge records its category, service period, net amount, due date, and VAT treatment; VAT-enabled organizations must explicitly choose standard-rated or out-of-scope treatment. Collections use the existing invoice, payment, and bank-settlement workflows. Multiple charges for the same lease and period are allowed because utilities and recoveries may be billed separately.
6. Ejari tracking is an operational, non-financial workflow. A draft or active lease may have one pending or registered Ejari record. Registration records the unique organization-scoped Ejari number, registration date, and expiry date. Renewal creates a new pending record linked to the immutable prior registration and marks that prior record renewed. Registered records expiring within 60 days appear in operational alerts and daily notifications. Applications, registrations, and renewals are tenant scoped and audited.
7. Security-deposit settlement is operational until ledger mappings are approved. Managers create one settlement per deposit, itemize categorized deductions with evidence references, and submit it to freeze the collected amount, deductions, and refundable balance. Submitted settlements are immutable and require organization-owner approval. Approval records no journal entry, payment, refund, deduction income, or forfeiture.
8. A finance manager may post the refundable balance of an owner-approved deposit settlement in AED by debiting Customer Deposits (2300) and crediting Payment Clearing (1190). The posting is idempotent and respects closed accounting periods. Its Payment Clearing credit is eligible for manual matching to an outbound bank CSV line; bank settlement then debits Payment Clearing and credits the linked bank asset account. Corrections use the existing journal and bank-settlement reversal controls. Deduction and forfeiture postings remain excluded.
9. A rent-arrears deduction must select an outstanding posted revenue invoice and cannot exceed its current balance. After owner approval of the settlement, a finance manager may post the deduction by debiting Customer Deposits (2300) and crediting Accounts Receivable (1100). The system records a non-cash `security_deposit_offset` allocation against the invoice, updates its payment status, prevents duplicate posting, and respects closed accounting periods. Other recovery and forfeiture deductions remain excluded.
10. Damage, cleaning, utility, and other recovery deductions require explicit VAT treatment when VAT is enabled. The entered deduction is gross. After owner settlement approval, a finance manager may debit Customer Deposits (2300), credit Recovery Income (4100) for the net amount, and credit Output VAT (2200) for standard-rated VAT; zero-rated, exempt, and out-of-scope deductions credit the full amount to Recovery Income. Posting is idempotent and respects closed periods.
11. Deposit forfeiture is a distinct deduction requiring a documented reason, evidence reference, and explicit VAT treatment when VAT is enabled. The amount is gross. After owner settlement approval, a finance manager may debit Customer Deposits (2300), credit Deposit Forfeiture Income (4200) for the net amount, and credit Output VAT (2200) when standard-rated. Posting is idempotent, tenant scoped, audited, and subject to closed-period controls.
12. Acceptance fixes: VAT preparation includes posted recovery/forfeiture journals and signed reversals by posting date. Rent offsets require a lease-contact or explicitly lease-linked invoice with detailed AED ledger posting; their dedicated reversal records a negative non-cash allocation to restore the invoice balance. Refund journals cannot be reversed while bank matched, and reversed clearing entries cannot be matched or settled. Manual owner sign-off is tracked in `docs/LEASING_ACCEPTANCE_CHECKLIST.md`.
