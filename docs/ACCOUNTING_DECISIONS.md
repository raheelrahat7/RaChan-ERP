# Accounting implementation decisions

Status: first accounting slice and AED finance mappings implemented after the 2026-09-15 instructions to resume and proceed. Historical journal summaries remain unallocated until an explicit mapping is approved.

Historical `journal_entries` store an event, subject, date, currency, and equal debit and credit totals without account allocations. New invoice and vendor-bill actions create detailed AED journal lines. Existing inventory and agreement migrations contain USD defaults, so historical currency must be inspected before any future ledger backfill.

## Implemented first accounting slice

1. Each organization owns an independent general ledger in AED with account types asset, liability, equity, income, and expense. Account codes are unique within an organization.
2. Posted journal entries contain at least two lines, positive debit or credit on each line, and equal debit and credit totals. Posted entries are immutable; errors are corrected with linked reversing entries.
3. Accounting periods belong to an organization. A closed period rejects new postings and reversals dated inside it. Owners and administrators can create and close periods; reopening is not exposed.
4. The Accounting UI exposes account maintenance, journal history, and a trial balance, scoped to the current organization and existing finance permissions. The trial balance and its CSV download can be limited to an as-of date and include detailed AED journal lines only; legacy journal summaries have no account allocations and are visibly marked as such.
5. Opening balances are separately posted as balanced AED journal lines dated inside an open accounting period. A source reference is required and unique within the organization, preventing duplicate imports. Reversals use the existing linked reversal-journal workflow. Historical summary-only entries require explicit mapping approval.
6. The account activity report lists dated, detailed AED journal lines for one organization-scoped account. It shows opening, in-range debit and credit, and closing net amounts using debit minus credit. Reversals appear as separate lines. A CSV download uses the same account and date range, includes the displayed totals, and escapes spreadsheet formulas. This is a ledger inspection view, not a finalized balance sheet or income statement.
7. The journal register lists all detailed AED journals with date and event filters and a matching line-level CSV export. It excludes historical summary-only entries, applies finance-view authorization and organization scoping, and escapes spreadsheet formulas in user-controlled fields.
8. The first financial-statements view contains an as-of balance sheet and a date-range profit-and-loss statement without comparative columns. The balance sheet uses natural account balances, requires an accounting period containing the as-of date, and shows profit before that period as prior accumulated results and profit within that period as current-period profit. Both statements use detailed AED journal lines only, provide date-matched spreadsheet-safe CSV downloads, and are draft internal reports until accountant review.
9. The outstanding-balances report reconstructs open receivables and payables at a selected date from invoice issue dates, vendor bill dates, and payments recorded through that date. It identifies customer and vendor parties, shows due dates and exact days overdue, and summarizes overdue amounts and document counts without imposing aging buckets. Users can view all, overdue, or not-overdue items; displayed totals and the spreadsheet-safe CSV follow the selected filter. It is organization scoped.
10. Each accounting period has a close-readiness report showing the detailed-ledger balance, unallocated legacy summaries, relevant drafts, and unresolved bank rows. A spreadsheet-safe CSV preserves the same totals and checks.
11. Period closing is blocked when detailed ledger debits and credits differ or unallocated legacy journal summaries exist in that period. Draft documents and unresolved bank rows remain advisory. Only an organization owner can reopen a closed period, including one with unresolved warnings; reopening clears the prior close metadata and creates an audit record.
    The Accounting period list exposes blocking readiness findings before close and disables its Close control when those findings exist; the domain action repeats the check for authoritative enforcement.
12. The finance audit trail lists organization-scoped `accounting.*` and `finance.*` audit events with actor, subject, timestamp, and recorded properties. Date, exact-event, and organization-member actor filters apply equally to the paginated view and spreadsheet-safe CSV export.
13. The Accounting landing page shows a selected-date finance overview using the same AED outstanding-balance rules as the detailed report, plus the accounting period containing that date and unresolved bank-row counts through it. The Accounting as-of filter drives both the trial balance and overview. Cards link to their detailed workflows, and a spreadsheet-safe CSV preserves the selected date.
14. The IAS 16 cost-model asset register requires an explicit asset class, AED cost, residual value, useful life in months, available-for-use date, and a source. Sources are limited to posted capital-asset vendor bills or opening-balance journals that debit Capital Assets (1500); cumulative registered cost cannot exceed the source amount. Recording a disposal date stops future depreciation eligibility but posts no disposal, derecognition, or gain/loss journal.
15. The monthly depreciation preview uses straight-line depreciation and prorates the monthly amount by eligible calendar days in the selected month. Eligibility starts on the available-for-use date, ends after the approved useful life, and stops at the recorded disposal date. Previewing is read-only and posts no journal.
16. A finance manager explicitly approves the visible monthly preview. Each eligible asset posts a separate AED journal dated at month end: debit Depreciation Expense (5100), credit Accumulated Depreciation (1590). Asset/month and journal source references prevent duplicates, an open accounting period is required, and accumulated routine depreciation cannot exceed cost less residual value.
17. A posted depreciation charge can be corrected only through its asset-register Reverse action, which creates a linked reversing journal in an open period and retains both approval and reversal history. The reversed asset/month becomes eligible for a corrected approval and repost.
18. A finance manager records one annual estimate review per asset and year. The review snapshots the current useful life, residual value, and straight-line method, records whether an estimate change or further impairment assessment is required, and creates an audit event. Reviews do not edit asset estimates, recalculate depreciation, or post journals; those actions remain pending separate approved prospective-change and impairment policies.
19. Annual review readiness lists assets in service during the selected year that have no review and completed reviews documenting an estimate change or impairment follow-up. A spreadsheet-safe CSV uses the same selected year and organization scope. Readiness is an operational review aid and does not block periods or create accounting entries.
20. A period ending on December 31 includes annual fixed-asset review readiness in its close-readiness page and CSV. Missing reviews and documented follow-ups remain informational checks; they do not change the approved ledger-only close blockers.
21. A finance manager may approve revised residual value and remaining useful life from a review marked `change_required`. The change becomes effective in the month after the later of the review month and the latest active posted depreciation month. Future straight-line charges use carrying amount at that date less revised residual value over revised remaining months; prior postings are retained and never recalculated.
22. After recording a disposal date and posting depreciation through that date, a finance manager approves disposal using an active linked AED bank account or Accounts Receivable (1100) for proceeds. The journal debits accumulated depreciation and proceeds, credits Capital Assets (1500), and posts the difference to Gain on Asset Disposal (4100) or Loss on Asset Disposal (5200). Corrections use a linked reversal from the asset register and permit corrected reposting.
23. A review requiring impairment assessment can support a finance-manager-approved impairment in an open period: debit Impairment Loss (5300), credit Accumulated Impairment (1580). The amount cannot exceed carrying value. It reduces the depreciable carrying basis prospectively from the following month over the remaining schedule; historical depreciation is unchanged. Corrections use a linked reversal, and later depreciation must be reversed first.
24. A finance manager may transfer an active fixed asset between organization properties or update its location, custodian, and asset class. Each transfer retains immutable before/after values, date, reason, approver, and audit metadata. It updates the asset register only and creates no journal entry because the current ledger uses shared fixed-asset cost and contra accounts.
25. VAT-enabled organizations store a 15-digit UAE TRN. New invoices and vendor bills require standard 5%, zero-rated, exempt, or out-of-scope classification. Invoice prices are VAT-exclusive; standard-rated invoices add 5% and credit Output VAT Payable (2200). Vendor bill totals are VAT-inclusive; recoverable standard-rate VAT is extracted and debited to Recoverable Input VAT (1250), while non-recoverable VAT remains in expense or asset cost. Existing records remain unclassified until manually updated.
26. VAT-enabled organizations select monthly or calendar-quarter reporting. A read-only VAT201 preparation report summarizes posted classified sales, output VAT, recoverable input VAT, and net payable or refundable VAT with transaction detail and matching CSV. It does not submit to the FTA, mark a return filed, or post VAT settlement journals.
27. Finance managers may save a VAT return preparation snapshot; an organization owner alone may mark it internally filed using an FTA reference and filing date. Filed snapshots are immutable. Managers may record audited post-filing output/input VAT adjustments with a discovery date and reason. A net VAT effect above AED 10,000 requires the voluntary-disclosure method; no FTA submission or settlement journal is automated.
28. A finance manager settles a filed VAT return through an active AED bank account. Payable returns debit Output VAT Payable (2200), credit Recoverable Input VAT (1250), and credit Bank for the net amount. Refundable returns clear both VAT accounts into VAT Refund Receivable (1260); the later receipt separately debits Bank and credits the receivable. Settlement includes post-filing adjustments and requires an open accounting period. An active settlement blocks further adjustments. Corrections reverse a refund receipt first when present, then reverse the settlement through linked journals; a corrected settlement may then be approved.
29. Corporate-tax preparation supports resident mainland companies and Qualifying Free Zone Persons using an organization tax year. It starts from detailed-ledger accounting profit and requires explicit exempt-income, non-deductible-expense, and other adjustments with notes. Mainland preparation applies 0% through AED 375,000 and 9% above it, or a per-period Small Business Relief election after confirming current and prior-period revenue does not exceed AED 3 million. QFZ preparation requires a conditions attestation and separate qualifying/non-qualifying income and revenue; the de-minimis ceiling is the lower of 5% of revenue and AED 5 million. When met, non-qualifying income is taxed at 9%; when failed, ordinary mainland rates apply. This is an internal preparation and does not file or post a tax provision.
30. An organization owner approves a prepared corporate-tax return and posts its provision in an open period: debit Corporate Tax Expense (5400), credit Corporate Tax Payable (2400). The owner separately records internal filing confirmation with its FTA reference and date. A finance manager pays a filed liability through an active linked AED bank account: debit Corporate Tax Payable, credit Bank. Provision and payment journals cannot be reversed from the general ledger; corrections reverse payment first and provision second through the return workflow, after which recalculation is allowed. Zero-tax returns retain approval and filing history without creating a zero-value journal.
31. Corporate-tax filing and payment are shown as due nine months after tax-period end. The preparation has a spreadsheet-safe CSV export containing the ledger starting point, adjustments, profile-specific calculations, status, filing reference, and due date. Tax-year close readiness reports missing preparation or approval, filing, and payment as advisory items; these do not change the approved ledger-only period-close blockers.

## Approved automatic mappings

- Invoice with revenue treatment: debit Accounts Receivable (1100), credit Revenue (4000).
- Invoice with refundable-deposit treatment: debit Accounts Receivable (1100), credit Customer Deposits (2300). New invoices require the treatment choice; historical drafts can be classified before posting.
- Customer payment against a mapped invoice: debit Undeposited Funds (1150), credit Accounts Receivable (1100).
- Vendor bill with operating-expense treatment: debit Operating Expense (5000), credit Accounts Payable (2100).
- Vendor bill with capital-asset treatment: debit Capital Assets (1500), credit Accounts Payable (2100). New bills require a treatment choice; historical and procurement-created drafts can be classified before posting.
- Vendor payment against a mapped bill: debit Accounts Payable (2100), credit Payment Clearing (1190).

The system creates these organization-scoped accounts on first use. A payment against a historical summary-only invoice or bill remains a summary-only journal; it is not mixed into the detailed trial balance. Existing summaries are not automatically backfilled; the historical mapping workflow requires explicit reviewed allocations.

## Approved first bank reconciliation workflow

Finance managers can create organization-scoped AED bank labels and import CSV rows with `transaction_id,date,description,amount`. IDs must be stable within a bank account; repeat imports are idempotent and conflicting repeats are rejected. Positive rows may be manually matched to equal customer-payment entries in Undeposited Funds (1150); negative rows may be matched to equal vendor-payment entries in Payment Clearing (1190). The operator can undo a match. Matching records an audited association only; it does not post a bank journal entry or move clearing balances. No bank API is used. The page shows up to 500 eligible clearing candidates for the visible statement rows.

Finance managers can link each bank label to an active AED asset ledger account, then explicitly approve settlement of a matched row. An incoming receipt debits the linked bank account and credits Undeposited Funds (1150); an outgoing vendor payment debits Payment Clearing (1190) and credits the linked bank account. Settlement uses the statement date and requires an open accounting period. A settled row cannot be unlinked or settled twice. Corrections use a dated reversing journal through the reconciliation screen; after reversal, the row may be unmatched or settled again. Bank settlement journals cannot be reversed from the general accounting screen. Statement rows can be filtered by bank label and match status.

## Customer credit notes — approved 2026-09-27

The owner approved the first customer-credit-note increment following the Phase 5 continuation proposal.

- Finance managers create reasoned drafts against organization-owned, posted AED revenue invoices with an original, unreversed detailed ledger posting. Refundable deposits, unallocated historical invoices, and draft invoices are excluded. Fully settled invoices are eligible only where actual direct cash was collected.
- Posting is a separate confirmation, requires an open accounting period, and rechecks the available credit under an invoice lock. Multiple drafts do not reserve balance. Credits above open receivables are capped at original unused invoice gross and eligible direct cash; total active credits cannot exceed the original invoice gross.
- Credits are VAT-inclusive amounts. They debit the original Revenue and, when applicable, Output VAT accounts and credit the original Accounts Receivable account. VAT uses the original posting ratio, with cumulative cent rounding across active credits; current organization tax settings do not recalculate the original treatment.
- Each posted note retains its amount, tax snapshot, reason, actor, date, and journal. Duplicate posting is rejected. Corrections require a dated, reasoned reversal through the credit-note workflow; general-ledger reversal cannot bypass the invoice balance update. Active credit notes block reversal of their original invoice journal. Active owner-approved cash refunds must be reversed before their source credit note can be reversed.
- Posting and reversal dates cannot be future dates or precede the source posting/latest credit-note activity. New credit postings must also follow existing payment/rent-offset activity; new rent offsets cannot predate existing credit-note activity. Reversals require an open period, restore the outstanding balance, and retain both journals and audit records.
- Invoice payment limits, rent-offset limits, dashboard totals, receivable reports/CSV, owner statements, and VAT preparation include credits and their dated reversals. Credits are shown separately from cash payments. The existing `paid` status means fully settled, including settlement by credit; the UI shows the credited amount explicitly.
- Historical reports count a credit from its posting date through the day before its reversal. VAT preparation includes negative credit activity and positive reversal activity on their respective dates. Previously filed return snapshots remain immutable; this increment does not automate amendments or filing.
- Supplier credit notes and budgets remain outside this increment.

## Customer cash refunds and supplier credit notes — approved 2026-09-27

The user approved these workflows under an owner-approval policy with maker-checker separation.

- Finance managers can submit a customer cash-refund request linked to a posted credit note. The organization owner must approve, reject, or reverse it, and cannot decide their own request. Approval rechecks cash capacity and the source credit note.
- Refund capacity is limited to direct customer receipts plus posted credits above the original invoice gross, less active prior refunds. Security-deposit offsets and rent offsets do not count as cash. Refund journals debit Accounts Receivable (1100) and credit Payment Clearing (1190). A matched bank statement item must be unmatched before workflow reversal.
- Finance managers can submit supplier credit notes against posted, detailed AED vendor bills. The organization owner approves, rejects, and reverses, subject to maker-checker separation. Total active notes cannot exceed the original bill gross. Credits reverse source AP and expense/asset lines, and recoverable input VAT is based on the original source ratio with cumulative cent rounding. Credits beyond a payable remain unapplied; cash payout to vendors is not included.
- Supplier credits reduce payable reports and future bill-payment capacity. VAT preparation includes dated negative purchase/input VAT rows and positive rows on reversal. Written accountant confirmation of this VAT and return treatment remains required before relying on the affected reports for filing.
- Both workflows require open periods, retain dates/reasons/actors/source links/journals, and create audit records. Generic journal reversal cannot bypass their workflow controls.

## Operating budgets — approved 2026-09-27

The user approved and implemented the first organization-wide operating budget increment in [FINANCE_BUDGET_POLICY_PROPOSAL.md](FINANCE_BUDGET_POLICY_PROPOSAL.md).

32. Each budget is organization-owned, calendar-year AED, and split by active expense ledger account into twelve monthly amounts. Finance managers can create drafts, save account lines incrementally and submit a frozen version for review.
33. An owner or administrator approves or rejects. The submitting user cannot approve or reject their own version. Rejection requires a reason and creates a new editable draft with the submitted amounts and history preserved.
34. Approval versions take effect on the first day of a current or future month, no earlier than the approval date. Replacements name the version they supersede and retain prior versions; reports use the version effective in each month. An approved replacement cannot change any month overlapping a closed accounting period.
35. Actuals are posted detailed AED ledger lines for expense accounts, debit minus credit, including reversals as posted activity. Unallocated historic journal summaries and unposted bills, orders, or estimates are excluded. The report and formula-safe CSV show monthly amounts, year-to-date budget/actual, remaining budget and actual-minus-budget variance. Missing accounts have zero budget and actual activity remains visible.
36. Budgets are advisory and read-only after approval. They create no journal entries and do not prevent posting, procurement, or maintenance actions. Property/project splits, commitments, revenue/cash forecasts, payroll, alerts, and carry-forward require separately approved policy.
37. Creation, submission, approval, rejection, and superseding actions are audited. Budget data and report actuals remain tenant scoped.

## Historical journal mapping — approved 2026-09-27

Owners have mapping approval authority by default and may explicitly grant/revoke it for organization members acting as accountants. This grant allows mapping decisions and reversals only; it does not change their organization role or other finance permissions. Removing a member revokes the grant. Finance managers submit reasoned allocations with source-evidence references, and cannot approve/reject their own proposals. The approved source snapshot and account lines are retained in the audit trail.

Allocations must use active organization accounts, balance exactly and equal the summary totals. Approval adds detailed lines to the original AED summary without changing its ID, reference, date, currency or totals. No second journal is posted at activation. Unapproved proposals are excluded from detailed-ledger reports, and one submitted proposal per source is allowed. Rejected proposals remain immutable and may be replaced by a new submission. Source/account eligibility is rechecked during approval.

The original date must lie in an open accounting period. Non-AED, already detailed, reversed and future journals are ineligible. Filed VAT coverage and any affected filed corporate-tax return block activation pending separate correction rules. Approved mappings can only be reversed through the mapping workflow by an owner or currently delegated approver. Reversal creates a dated linked journal in an open period, keeps original lines and does not re-open the source for remapping. Matched bank rows must be unmatched first. Written external statement/tax sign-off remains a separate review gate.

## Remaining roadmap decisions — 2026-09-27

The owner selected operational weighted-average spare-part reporting with no automatic stock journals. Receipt total costs are explicit, each part uses one currency, organization-wide pooled value is preserved by store transfers, and unused returns restore their original issue value. Missing or inconsistent costing remains visibly unvalued rather than being silently assigned a zero cost.

Construction BOQ rates and contractor claims represent the fixed gross bill amount, inclusive of VAT where applicable. Progress controls claimable quantity. A different owner approves the requester’s claim before a finance manager creates a bill draft using the existing expense/capital and VAT treatments. Owner approval does not post the bill. Existing bill posting and accounting-period controls remain mandatory.

Vendor cash refunds mean money received back from the vendor, not an outbound payout. Finance requests a dated cash return against a posted supplier credit; a different owner approves it within both receipt-date and current actual overpayment capacity. The journal debits payment clearing 1190 and credits accounts payable 2100. Dedicated reasoned reversals use an open accounting period and reject bank-matched source journals. Active cash returns prevent reversal of supporting credit/payment records until the return is reversed. No new tax-policy or regulatory compliance certification follows from these local workflows.
